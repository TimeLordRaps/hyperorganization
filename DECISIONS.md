## D01. A price is the surprisal of a compromise

**Tag:** FRAME

**Choice.** Replace money by bits. The price of compromising a party is -log2 of the probability that it is compromised; a gate costs the cheapest total price of a set of parties that passes it; a stake is the log2 of how many independent attempts a prize attracts.

**Not chosen.**
- Keep currency units: rejected, because you asked for no monetary construction and the units are arbitrary.
- Use probability itself as the price: rejected, because probabilities multiply, so gate costs would not add and hyperethics' cover search would not apply.
- Effort or time as the price: rejected, because it brings a wage back in.

**Expectation.** Someone who recomputes hyperethics' gate costs with prices read as bits gets the same numbers, and the exact probability of taking each gate lies between 2^-cost and (number of minimal covers) x 2^-cost.

**Refuter.** One gate whose cost differs from hyperethics', or one gate whose exact probability falls outside that bracket.

**World witness.** For a real body: the frequency with which a coalition of seats is compromised together should equal the product of its members' frequencies when there is no common cause. Needs incident data; none is held here.

**Where.** surprisal.gate_bits, sandwich_gate; tests/test_surprisal.py; data/golden_l2_gate_bits.json

**Model check:** HOLDS_IN_MODEL — a price in bits reproduces hyperethics' gate costs and brackets the probability
- 20 gates in 8 systems equal hyperethics gate_cost (commit a13770716f57)
- 20 gates: 2^-cost <= exact probability <= union bound, in exact rationals

## D02. A common principal is a measurable loss of surprisal

**Tag:** FRAME

**Choice.** Treat shared command as positive association between compromises, measured by additivity_gap = bits(product of marginals) - bits(joint), and count roots, not names.

**Not chosen.**
- Treat every seat as independent: rejected, because it overstates the price of any set of seats that answer to one principal, which is hyperethics' common-principal cause.
- Give correlation a free parameter rho: rejected, because rho has no observable meaning and a shared principal does.

**Expectation.** For a principal at 3 bits commanding two seats, the gap is exactly 3 bits: the same discount hyperethics finds when one Boss at price 3 replaces two seats at price 3 each (gate cost 6 falls to 3).

**Refuter.** A shared-principal system whose gap differs from the cheaper-cover discount.

**World witness.** Compromises across the seats of one body that share an appointer or a funder: their joint frequency exceeds the product of the marginals by the predicted amount. Needs data.

**Where.** surprisal.additivity_gap, common_principal_joint; tests/test_surprisal.py

**Model check:** HOLDS_IN_MODEL — a common principal is a measurable loss of surprisal
- a principal at 3 bits commanding two seats: additivity gap 3.000000 bits
- hyperethics' cheaper cover through the same command: 6 bits apart, 3 together, discount 3

## D03. Independence understates capture risk when compromise is positively associated

**Tag:** FRAME

**Choice.** Keep the independent-seat (mean-field) model for thresholds and report the direction of its error instead of hiding it.

**Not chosen.**
- Solve a specific ring or graph exactly: deferred. Exact enumeration reaches about a dozen bodies, below any size where a threshold is sharp.
- Choose a correlation parameter: rejected, as in D02.

**Expectation.** Group the seats of a body under shared principals, keeping every seat's marginal fixed. With the quorum above the mean number compromised, capture becomes MORE likely than the independent formula says; with the quorum below it, LESS likely. A witness running the grouped computation sees both directions.

**Refuter.** A grouping, with the quorum above the mean, whose capture probability is lower than the independent one (positive association cannot do that).

**World witness.** Positive association across seats of real bodies, measured. The thresholds in this package are then upper bounds on the pressure a body tolerates, not estimates. Needs data.

**Where.** surprisal.capture_probability, Block; quorum.tail; tests/test_surprisal.py

**Model check:** HOLDS_IN_MODEL — independence understates capture risk when the quorum is above the mean
- 5 groupings with quorum above the mean: grouped capture >= independent, understated by up to 3.967 bits
- worked case: two groups of three seats, quorum 4, x = 1/4: grouped 1/16, independent 0.0376
- below the mean (x = 2/3, quorum 2): grouped 0.9630 < independent 0.9822, the direction reverses

## D04. Existence is membership in the viability kernel, and persistence is not goodness

**Tag:** FRAME

**Choice.** Existence means: the system can keep itself inside a safe set for ever against any nature. The optimal structure is the least restriction that preserves this, and nothing more.

**Not chosen.**
- Existence as expected lifetime: rejected, because it needs a probability on nature and loses the worst-case guarantee.
- Existence as a utility to maximise: rejected, because it builds in a value, which is the family's open GC-5.

**Expectation.** The greatest law permits some stable arrangements a participant would call tyranny. A witness should find, in a game with a stable bad state, that the state is in the kernel and the move into it is permitted. Anything that excludes it is an extra axiom, not a consequence of existence.

**Refuter.** A derivation, inside the game, that removes the stable bad state from the kernel without adding a welfare term.

**World witness.** None available: that stable bad regimes exist is historical, not a witness for or against this model. Why persistence should count as optimality is D16.

**Where.** viability.kernel, greatest_law; tests/test_viability.py

**Model check:** HOLDS_IN_MODEL — existence alone does not exclude a stable bad state
- kernel = ['fair', 'tyranny']
- the greatest law at 'fair' permits ['drift', 'stay'] and forbids ['collapse']: persistence is blind to who is better off

## D05. The law is the greatest sustaining law: unique, canonical, naming no one

**Tag:** FORM

**Choice.** Define the optimal law as the pointwise-greatest assignment of permitted actions that keeps the system in its kernel for ever. It forbids exactly what ends existence, permits everything else, and mentions no participant.

**Not chosen.**
- A voted or weighted law: rejected, because the game contains no weights to vote with.
- The smallest law that works (permit only what is needed): rejected, because it is the maximum restriction, not the least.

**Expectation.** On every finite game and every start state, a law is sustaining exactly when everything it permits at the states it reaches lies in the greatest law; renaming states, actions or nature choices renames the law the same way, so it cannot favour a participant the game does not already single out.

**Refuter.** A game, a law and a start where the two disagree, or a renaming that changes the law.

**World witness.** None needed: a theorem of the model, checked by enumerating every law on the sample.

**Where.** viability.greatest_law, sustaining, all_laws, Game.relabel; tests/test_viability.py

**Model check:** HOLDS_IN_MODEL — the optimal law is the unique greatest sustaining law and names no participant
- 24 kernel states over 20 random games: the greatest law sustains existence from each, by brute force
- 2592 (law, start) pairs: the module's verdict equals brute force, and every sustaining law lies inside the greatest
- 10 games renamed: the law renames with them

## D06. Free will is the freedom to take any permitted action: a law limits what is permitted, not what is chosen

**Tag:** FRAME

**Choice.** Model the choice among permitted actions as taking each with positive probability (full support), and nature likewise.

**Not chosen.**
- Best-response agents: rejected, because an agent that sees a fatal move and avoids it needs no law, which would make the question 'which laws are needed' empty.
- An adversarial agent: rejected, because that is nature.

**Expectation.** Survival with probability one exactly when the law is sustaining. One permission outside the greatest law at a state the system reaches puts survival strictly below one: exempting an author from a prohibition that protects existence is fatal. This derives, inside the model, the L2 clause exempt-reach.

**Refuter.** A sustaining law with survival below 1, or a non-sustaining law with survival 1.

**World witness.** Exposure: an actor with unconstrained access to a catastrophic action should use it with probability growing toward one with the time it holds the access. Testable on institutional data; not tested here.

**Where.** viability.survival_probability; tests/test_viability.py

**Model check:** HOLDS_IN_MODEL — under full support, survival is certain iff the law is sustaining, and an exemption is fatal
- 48 starts: the greatest law survives with probability exactly 1
- 22 single permissions added outside it: survival below 1 each time

## D07. Corrigibility is staying where an overseer can still bring the system home

**Tag:** FRAME

**Choice.** Give the game a home set and overseer correction moves, and require the system to stay inside the set from which home is reachable. A move that leaves it is irreversible.

**Not chosen.**
- Utility indifference or an off-switch game: deferred, because it needs payoffs, which this package avoids.
- Require every action to be reversible: rejected, because it forbids learning; only reachability of home matters.

**Expectation.** The corrigible kernel lies inside the kernel, strictly inside for a system that can survive by passing through a point of no return, and the corrigible law forbids exactly the move through it.

**Refuter.** A game whose corrigible kernel is not inside its kernel, or where the corrigible law permits a move out of the home-reachable set.

**World witness.** L2's irreversible-without-ex-ante-check clause: bodies holding irreversible powers with no prior check should show worse recoverability. Needs data.

**Where.** viability.reach_home, kernel(corrigible=True); tests/test_viability.py

**Model check:** HOLDS_IN_MODEL — corrigibility is staying where an overseer can bring the system home
- cul-de-sac game: kernel ['p', 'q', 'r'], corrigible kernel ['p', 'r']; the corrigible law at p permits ['stay'] and forbids the move to the dead end
- 150 random games with a home: corrigible kernel inside the kernel always, strictly inside in 47

## D09. Revitalization is self-repair, and redundancy cannot replace it

**Tag:** FRAME

**Choice.** Seats are attacked at rate lambda and repaired at rate nu, repair happens only while the body still holds (the body repairs itself), and the body is lost when q seats are down.

**Not chosen.**
- Repair by an outside agent: rejected, because an outside repairer is an exempt exterior (L2's externally-held).
- Repair always available: rejected, because it removes the point of no return.

**Expectation.** Three regimes by rho = lambda/(lambda+nu) against theta = q/m: life grows like exp(m D)/sqrt(m), D the Bernoulli KL divergence, if rho < theta; tends to ln(rho/(rho-theta))/(lambda+nu) whatever m is if rho > theta; grows like (1/2)(ln 2m + gamma)/(lambda+nu) at rho = theta = 1/2. A body that is never repaired dies in ln(1/(1-theta))/lambda however many seats it has.

**Refuter.** Life that grows with m past the margin, or does not grow exponentially inside it.

**World witness.** Institutions without member renewal (unbounded terms) should fail on a time scale that does not depend on their size. Needs data.

**Where.** renewal.mean_lifetime, log_mean_lifetime, exponent; tests/test_renewal.py

**Model check:** HOLDS_IN_MODEL — revitalization is self-repair, and redundancy cannot replace it
- inside the margin (rho .2, theta .5): ln life - m D + (1/2) ln m is 1.4310 at m=1600 and 1.4304 at m=3200: life grows as exp(m D)
- past the margin (rho .8, theta .5): life 0.9804 at m=3200 against the limit ln(rho/(rho-theta))/rate = 0.9808
- no repair, 2000 seats, half needed: life 0.6929 (ln 2 = 0.6931) however many seats
- at the margin: quadrupling the seats adds 0.6931 (predicted (1/2) ln 4 = 0.6931)

## D10. A checker that commands and a checker that only vetoes are one equation in dual variables, and vetoing is worth more

**Tag:** FRAME

**Choice.** Model an oversight link as COMMAND (compromise propagates: p = pi + (1-pi) T(p)) or VETO (compromise needs both: p = pi T(p)).

**Not chosen.**
- Only COMMAND, hyperethics' control relation: rejected, because it makes every check as weak as its commander.
- A blended link with a mixing parameter: deferred; the two extremes are the clean cases.

**Expectation.** 1 - G_command(1-s) = G_veto*(s) exactly, with the dual quorum; for a self-dual quorum the two critical pressures sum to exactly 1; and the veto threshold exceeds the command threshold at every odd majority.

**Refuter.** A grid point where the identity fails, or an odd majority where veto does not exceed command.

**World witness.** Comparative: oversight bodies that appoint and remove (command) should fail at lower attack pressure than ones that can only block. Needs data.

**Where.** loop.step, threshold; tests/test_loop.py

**Model check:** HOLDS_IN_MODEL — command and veto are one equation in dual variables, and vetoing is worth more
- duality 1 - G_command(1 - s) = G_veto*(s) exact on 168 grid points
- self-dual quorums at m in (3, 5, 7, 11, 21, 51, 101): critical pressures sum to 1 and veto exceeds command
- m = 5, q = 3: command 0.1651, veto 0.8349, a factor of 5.06

## D11. The balanced quorum is the odd majority, critical at exactly one half

**Tag:** FRAME

**Choice.** Call a quorum balanced when capturing and blocking are equally hard (q = m-q+1). That makes the quorum map self-dual.

**Not chosen.**
- Weight safety above liveness (q above the majority): kept as the general case; the balanced point is the equal weighting.
- Minimise expected loss under a stated prior: deferred, because it needs a prior.

**Expectation.** T(1/2) = 1/2 exactly for every odd majority; the price of such a council at x = 1/2 is exactly one bit; the critical point moves down when the quorum is lowered and up when it is raised.

**Refuter.** A non-majority quorum critical at 1/2, or a majority that is not.

**World witness.** None: a theorem of the model. Whether real bodies should be balanced is a value choice, and open.

**Where.** quorum.critical_point, tail; surprisal.council_bits; tests/test_quorum.py

**Model check:** HOLDS_IN_MODEL — the balanced quorum is the odd majority, critical at exactly one half
- odd majorities m = 3..21: T(1/2) = 1/2 exactly and the council's price at x = 1/2 is exactly 1 bit
- m = 9: the critical point rises with the quorum, q=3: 0.1577, q=4: 0.3219, q=5: 0.5000, q=6: 0.6781, q=7: 0.8423

## D12. Closing the loop costs little while healthy and everything near the threshold

**Tag:** FRAME

**Choice.** Report the gap between a finite hierarchy with an unchecked apex and the folded loop (the closing price), and the delay before collapse past the threshold.

**Not chosen.**
- Report only fixed points: rejected, because it hides the finite-time behaviour real institutions live in.

**Expectation.** At pi = 0.1 for a 5-seat, 3-quorum command loop, one level of oversight is already within 2% of the loop's limit. Just past the threshold the number of levels before collapse grows like (pi - pi*)^(-1/2): 100x closer, 10x longer.

**Refuter.** A closing price that is not small in the healthy phase, or a delay exponent other than -1/2.

**World witness.** Critical slowing down: near a saddle-node, recovery from perturbation slows and variance rises (the generic early-warning signature of a critical transition, recalled from the literature, not checked here). Seeing it in an institution is the witness.

**Where.** loop.closing_price, levels_to_cross; tests/test_loop.py

**Model check:** HOLDS_IN_MODEL — closing the loop costs little while healthy and everything near the threshold
- 5 seats, quorum 3, pi = 0.1: one level of oversight is within 0.00242 of the loop's limit
- 51 seats, quorum 26: 155 levels before collapse at 1e-4 past the threshold, 1568 at 1e-6: x10.12 for 100x closer, i.e. (pi-pi*)^(-1/2)

## D13. A phase is an ordinal rank; transfinite ranks appear only when the structure nests

**Tag:** FRAME

**Choice.** Represent the progress of a self-closing process as a rank in a well-ordered set: natural numbers and omega for a finite game, ordinals below epsilon_0 for processes with hereditary structure (Goodstein).

**Not chosen.**
- Real-valued Lyapunov functions: kept for smooth dynamics, but they carry no proof-theoretic information.
- Surreal or hyperreal phases: rejected, because nothing here needs them.

**Expectation.** Every rank is an ordinal comparing as integers do; ranks of finite games stay below omega+1; Goodstein from 4 starts at omega^omega and its ordinals strictly descend for hundreds of terms while the first terms are 4, 26, 41, 60, 83, 109.

**Refuter.** A descent that fails to strictly decrease, or a finite game with a rank that is neither a number nor omega.

**World witness.** None: this is a representation. Whether it is the right shared one across the hyperfields is D18.

**Where.** ordinal.Ordinal, goodstein_sequence, check_descent; tests/test_ordinal.py

**Model check:** HOLDS_IN_MODEL — a phase is an ordinal rank; transfinite ranks appear when the structure nests
- viability ranks compare as the ordinals 0 < 1 < 2 < 7 < ω
- 20 random games: 29 finite ranks (each at most the number of states) and 48 of rank ω; nothing between

## D14. Protection is the weakest link, and with no sovereign there is no finite rank

**Tag:** FORM

**Choice.** Model a constitution as rules each governed by another rule, with a price for the procedure each defines. Protection of a rule is the cheapest procedure above it; authority height is the number of governors above it.

**Not chosen.**
- Protection as the strength of the rule's own procedure: rejected, because it ignores the route through the amendment clause (Ross).
- A rank assigned by hand: rejected, because on a loop none exists.

**Expectation.** A finite height anywhere means some rule nothing governs; with every rule governed, every height is omega; and an entrenched clause amendable by a cheap clause is protected by the cheap clause.

**Refuter.** A governance graph where every rule is governed and some height is finite, or a clause protected by more than its cheapest ancestor.

**World witness.** None needed: a theorem of the model, checked over every graph on up to four rules and against an independent shortest-path search.

**Where.** entrenchment.protection, authority_height, cycles; tests/test_entrenchment.py

**Model check:** HOLDS_IN_MODEL — protection is the weakest link, and with no sovereign there is no finite rank
- all 700 governance graphs on up to 4 rules: a finite height anywhere means something is ungoverned; with every rule governed, every height is ω
- Ross: a clause worth 100 bits amendable by a 5-bit clause is protected by 5 bits

## D15. The seven L2 clauses map onto parameters of these models; one is derived, five are modelled and one is not

**Refuter.** A clause whose model-side quantity disagrees with the hyperethics number it should equal.

**World witness.** None needed for the arithmetic. Whether the model describes any real institution is hyperethics' open nd-adequacy-of-the-capture-model.

## D17. That physical reality adheres to an optimal hyperethical structure cannot be witnessed here

**Tag:** HYPER

**Choice.** Record the proposal that physical stability conditions are kernel conditions and physical tipping points are saddle-node thresholds of closure maps, as a proposal, and attach no check to it.

**Not chosen.**
- State it as a result of the models: rejected, because nothing here touches a physical measurement.
- Drop it: rejected, because it is the part of your question about reality, and it has a stated form that a measurement could refute.

**Expectation.** A bistable physical system whose microscopic parameters are measured independently should have a measured tipping point equal to the threshold computed from them, with no free fit.

**Refuter.** A system with independently measured parameters whose tipping point disagrees with the computed threshold.

**World witness.** No witness is available at the physics level. Bistability, hysteresis and critical slowing down are observed in real systems; matching a threshold to measured microscopic parameters is the missing step.

**Where.** loop.threshold; L2 and L0 in hyperethics

## D18. Hyperoptimization as the closing catalyst across the family is a claim to test on the family's own data

**Tag:** OPEN

**Choice.** Record the claim as open, with the test it implies, instead of asserting it.

**Not chosen.**
